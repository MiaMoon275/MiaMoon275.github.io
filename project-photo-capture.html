Time-critical workflow · Sports organization

5,600 membership photos, one clear process.

A practical capture system that connected each photo with the right member and kept a high-volume production run moving under a fixed deadline.

The challenge

A large production task with little room for mix-ups.

Since the Club installed a new turnstile system, it became necessary to update the membership cards with current photos to ensure smooth access for all members. The manual process was too slow: receive pictures by thousands of members, edit them manually and match them to the correct membership records. With that, the club faced a significant bottleneck in updating membership until the seasonal opening.

The club asked me to design a system to increase speed of this process. Photos needed to be captured in a standardized format, correctly associated with the right membership records, and prepared for the next production step without errors. The Club prepared a dedicated capture station to facilitate this process.

My contribution

I designed the process around the people at the capture station.

I built a focused application with minimal features. A clear button for photo capture initiates the pricess. Once the photo is taken, the employee could enter either the membership number or the members name, a dynamic table would suggest members while typing, allowing for quick and accurate selection. After confirmation, the system automatically associates the photo with the correct membership record.

Additional Storaging System

Support for capturing photos without immediate server access

An additional storaging system allowed the club to capture pictures at places without access to the SQL Server. This system temporarily stored the captured photos locally and synchronized them with the central SQL Server once a connection was available. This was especially useful to capture photos during training sessions or external events, saving the members from having to go to the main office unnecessarily.

The outcome

A repeatable flow that supported the full capturing run.

The solution enabled the organization to process the required photos and prepare 5,600 membership cards. Staff could concentrate on the people in front of them instead of file names and folders, while consistent handling reduced follow-up work.

This project shows how I respond to time pressure: simplify the workflow, focus on operational reliability, and deliver a tool that non-technical users can adopt quickly.
